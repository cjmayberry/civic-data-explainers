## What this tracks  
This dataset records wildlife crossing structures in Nevada designed to help animals safely cross roads, including their type, location, and target species.

## Why it matters to you  
If you're driving through rural Nevada on US Route 93 near 10 Mile Summit, this data tells you where deer and other large animals are most likely to cross the highway. Knowing these locations helps you stay alert for wildlife, especially at dawn and dusk when collisions are most common. For ranchers, it shows where fencing might need reinforcement to guide livestock away from dangerous crossings.

## How to read this data  
**StructureType** — The kind of crossing, like "Lrg Steel Culvert" (a large tunnel) or other designs that let animals pass under roads.  
**FocalSpecies** — Which animals the crossing was built for, such as "Lrg Ungulates" (deer, elk, or similar hooved animals).  
**RoadName** — The highway where the crossing is located, like "US93" for U.S. Route 93.  
**Status** — Whether the project is "Complete" or under construction.  

## Try it yourself  
Check if your regular driving route passes a wildlife crossing by searching [Nevada's mapping portal](https://gis.nv.gov/) for "US93" or another highway in the RoadName field. Slow down when you approach these zones.
