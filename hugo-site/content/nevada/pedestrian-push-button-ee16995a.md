## What this tracks  
This dataset records the locations and compliance status of ADA-accessible pedestrian push buttons at crosswalks across Nevada.

## Why it matters to you  
Knowing which crosswalk buttons are ADA-compliant helps wheelchair users plan accessible routes in Nevada. The data tells you whether a button requires excessive force to activate (which could strand someone with limited strength) or if it provides audible walk signals (critical for visually impaired pedestrians). Businesses can also check compliance before hosting events near major intersections.

## How to read this data  
**ComplianceStatus** — Whether the button meets ADA standards, with values like "Compliant" or sample notes like "Force 1 Lbs."  
**Button_Height** — Installation height in inches (sample: 40.75"), which must be within reach range for wheelchair users.  
**cross_time** — How many seconds pedestrians get to cross (sample note: "Cross time: 63 seconds").  
**aud_walk_ind_type** — Whether the button provides audible cues (blank in samples means unknown/no data).  

## Try it yourself  
Check the crosswalk button nearest to your workplace by searching Nevada's ADA map for your street address to see if it's marked "Compliant" and has audible signals.
