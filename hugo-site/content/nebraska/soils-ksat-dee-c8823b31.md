## What this tracks  
This dataset measures how quickly water moves through saturated soil in Nebraska, expressed in inches per hour.  

## Why it matters to you  
If you’re planning to install a septic system, build a basement, or plant crops, this data tells you whether your property’s soil drains water quickly or slowly. Fast-draining soil (high Ksat) may need different foundations or drainage systems than slow-draining soil. For farmers, knowing Ksat helps predict irrigation needs and avoid waterlogging crops.  

## How to read this data  
**AREASYMBOL** — Identifies the soil survey area (e.g., "NE165").  
**KSATCLASS_DCP** — The soil’s saturated hydraulic conductivity (e.g., 8.8 inches per hour). Higher numbers mean faster drainage.  
**COMPPCT_R** — The percentage of the map unit represented by this soil component (e.g., 50%).  

## Try it yourself  
Check the Ksat value for your property using the [USDA Web Soil Survey](https://websoilsurvey.sc.egov.usda.gov/) by entering your Nebraska address.
