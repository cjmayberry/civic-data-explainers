## What this tracks  
This dataset records active and historical wildland fire incidents across Tennessee, including their locations, sizes, causes, and containment status.

## Why it matters to you  
If you're considering buying property near wooded areas in Tennessee, this data shows which locations have experienced wildfires historically. Hikers and campers can check recent fire activity before planning trips to state parks or national forests. Farmers and landowners can identify whether their county (like the sample "Los Angeles County") has higher fire risk based on past incidents.

## How to read this data  
**FireCause** — Why the fire started, with values like "Unknown" (sample value) or natural/accidental causes.  
**DiscoveryAcres** — Size when first detected, like the sample 0.1-acre fire.  
**POOCounty** — County where fire occurred, such as "Los Angeles" in the sample.  
**FireDiscoveryDateTime** — When the fire was first reported (sample: 1582922740000, which converts to February 29, 2020).

## Where this leaves you  
This dataset doesn't let you look anything up by your address, but it's still a window into how the state operates. Read it to understand the scope of the program and what the official records contain; the state's own interactive map (linked on this page) shows the locations involved.
