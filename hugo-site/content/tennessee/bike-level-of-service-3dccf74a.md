## What this tracks
This dataset measures how bicycle-friendly major roads are throughout Tennessee, rating each road segment with letter grades from A to F.

## Why it matters to you
If you're planning a bike commute or recreational ride in Tennessee, this data tells you which roads are safest and most comfortable for cycling before you head out. A road with an "A" rating likely has bike lanes and lower traffic, while an "F" rating means heavy traffic with no bike infrastructure. Business owners near highly-rated bike routes can better understand their potential cycling customer base and plan bike-friendly amenities accordingly.

## How to read this data
**RTE_NME** — The actual road name, like "CLINCH AVE." **SPD_LMT** — Posted speed limit in mph, with sample values like 45 mph. **AB_BA_OVERALL_BLOS_SCORE** — The combined bicycle safety rating for both directions of travel, ranging from A (best) to F (worst), with sample values like "B". **THRU_LANES** — Number of through traffic lanes, with sample values like 4 lanes, which affects how stressful the road feels for cyclists.

## Where this leaves you  
This dataset doesn't let you look anything up by your address, but it's still a window into how the state operates. Read it to understand the scope of the program and what the official records contain; the state's own interactive map (linked on this page) shows the locations involved.
