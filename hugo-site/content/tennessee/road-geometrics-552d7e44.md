## What this tracks
This dataset records the physical characteristics of Tennessee state roads, including speed limits, number of lanes, and terrain type for specific segments.

## Why it matters to you
This data helps you understand road conditions before you travel. If you're planning a route through Tennessee, you can check whether a road has steep terrain that might slow down trucks or RVs, or see if a narrow two-lane road might cause delays during peak hours. Business owners shipping goods can use this information to plan delivery routes that avoid challenging terrain or roads with access restrictions.

## How to read this data
**SPD_LMT** — the posted speed limit for that road segment, shown in miles per hour like 30 or 55. **NBR_LANES** — total number of lanes in both directions, with values like 2 for a standard two-lane road or 4 for a divided highway. **TY_TERRAIN** — describes the landscape as "Flat," "Rolling," or "Mountainous," which affects driving difficulty and fuel consumption. **DRCT_ONE_WAY** — indicates traffic flow, typically showing "Two-Way Traffic" for most roads.

## Where this leaves you  
This dataset doesn't let you look anything up by your address, but it's still a window into how the state operates. Read it to understand the scope of the program and what the official records contain; the state's own interactive map (linked on this page) shows the locations involved.
