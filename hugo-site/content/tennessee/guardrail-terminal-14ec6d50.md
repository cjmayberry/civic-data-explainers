## What this tracks  
This dataset records the locations and types of guardrail end terminals installed on Tennessee highways, interstates, and state park roads.

## Why it matters to you  
If you're planning a road trip or commute through Tennessee, this data tells you where guardrail safety features begin and end — critical for understanding roadside safety along your route. Small trucking businesses can verify if specific highway segments have modern guardrail terminals (like ET-Plus models) when assessing risk for insurance or route planning. Residents near high-traffic exits can check if their local ramps have up-to-date safety hardware.

## How to read this data  
**FEAT_DESC** — The guardrail terminal model, e.g., "Type 38 - Extruded Terminal (ET-Plus)" shows crash-tested designs.  
**LOC_DESC** — Placement direction like "Left" indicates which side of the road the terminal is on.  
**LOG_MILE** — The mile marker position (e.g., 0.115 means just past mile 0).  
**ROUTE** — Highway identifier like "I0240" for I-240 in Shelby County.

## Try it yourself  
Check guardrail terminals near your regular exit by searching [TDOT's map](https://www.tn.gov/tdot.html) for your route number (like I-240) and cross-referencing the exit number.
