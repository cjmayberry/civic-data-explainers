## What this tracks  
This dataset maps Tennessee's Conservation Opportunity Areas (COAs) and identifies Species of Greatest Conservation Need (SGCN) for the state's 2025 wildlife protection plan.  

## Why it matters to you  
If you own land near a COA, this data tells you whether your property falls in a priority zone for habitat protection or restoration funding. Farmers can check if their fields border high-priority streams (like the 86 miles tracked here) to qualify for agricultural conservation programs. Hikers and hunters can use it to find areas with vulnerable species populations before planning trips.  

## How to read this data  
**COA** — The protected area's name, like "South Fork of the Forked Deer River."  
**AveragePriorityScore** — A number (e.g., 32) showing how urgently the area needs conservation, with higher scores meaning greater priority.  
**MilesHighPriorityStreams** — Total miles of at-risk waterways in the COA (sample: 86 miles).  
**document_url** — Links to detailed plans (e.g., a PDF attachment) explaining conservation strategies for each area.

## Where this leaves you  
This dataset doesn't let you look anything up by your address, but it's still a window into how the state operates. Read it to understand the scope of the program and what the official records contain; the state's own interactive map (linked on this page) shows the locations involved.
