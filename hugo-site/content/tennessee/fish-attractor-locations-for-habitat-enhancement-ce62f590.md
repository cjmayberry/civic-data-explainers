## What this tracks  
This dataset maps locations where Tennessee wildlife agencies have placed fish attractors like PVC pipes or Christmas trees to improve fishing habitats.

## Why it matters to you  
Anglers can use this to find prime fishing spots where fish naturally congregate around these structures. If you're planning a fishing trip to Kentucky Lake or Lick Creek, checking these locations could mean the difference between catching dinner and going home empty-handed. Local bait shops and marinas near these attractors may also see increased business from informed anglers.

## How to read this data  
**WaterBody** — The lake or river containing the attractor (e.g., "Kentucky Lake").  
**Artificial/Natural_** — What material attracts fish (e.g., "PVC Pipe" or "Christmas Tree").  
**DepthRange** — How deep the attractor sits (e.g., "5" feet).  
**Marker** — Whether the spot is visibly marked (e.g., "Y" for yes).

## Where this leaves you  
This dataset doesn't let you look anything up by your address, but it's still a window into how the state operates. Read it to understand the scope of the program and what the official records contain; the state's own interactive map (linked on this page) shows the locations involved.
