## What this tracks  
This dataset measures the depth, elevation, and water quality of the Dakota Aquifer beneath Kansas.  

## Why it matters to you  
If you're drilling a well or planning agricultural irrigation in Kansas, this data tells you how deep to dig to reach the aquifer and whether the water contains high levels of dissolved solids (salts). Farmers can compare aquifer depth across regions to estimate drilling costs, while rural homeowners can check if their area has naturally harder water before installing filtration systems.  

## How to read this data  
**ALTITUDE_F** — Elevation (in feet above sea level) where the aquifer begins. Sample value: 1300 means the top of the aquifer is 1,300 ft above sea level at that location.  
**DK_BASE_** — Depth (in feet) to the base of the aquifer layer. Sample value: 54 indicates the aquifer’s bottom is 54 ft below its top elevation.  

## Try it yourself  
Check the aquifer depth near your property using [Kansas Geological Survey’s interactive map](https://www.kgs.ku.edu/) to estimate well-drilling feasibility.
