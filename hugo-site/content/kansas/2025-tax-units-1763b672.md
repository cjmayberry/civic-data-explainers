## What this tracks  
This dataset records the official boundaries and certification status of tax units across Kansas for 2025.

## Why it matters to you  
If you own property in Kansas, this data confirms which tax unit your land falls under — determining your local mill levy rates and which services (like schools or fire districts) your taxes fund. Business owners can verify they're being assessed correctly by cross-checking their location against these boundaries before appealing property valuations. The "REPORT2025" field specifically tells you whether your area's tax unit changed since last year, which could impact your future tax bills.

## How to read this data  
**TAXUNIT** — A unique code for each tax district (e.g., "194" in Osage County).  
**COUNTY** — The county where the tax unit is located (e.g., "Osage County").  
**REPORT2025** — Indicates if boundaries changed ("No Changes" means your taxes will likely follow last year's structure).  

## Try it yourself  
Check if your property's tax unit changed for 2025 by comparing your latest tax statement's TAXUNIT code against the [Kansas geospatial catalog](https://archivehub.kansasgis.org/).
