## What this tracks  
This dataset measures the depth, elevation, and extent of the Dakota Aquifer beneath Kansas, including water quality indicators like total dissolved solids.

## Why it matters to you  
If you rely on well water in Kansas, this data tells you how deep you’ll need to drill to reach the Dakota Aquifer and whether the water quality is suitable for drinking or irrigation. Farmers can use it to plan crop irrigation systems, and homeowners can check if their property sits above a viable groundwater source before investing in a well.  

## How to read this data  
**ALTITUDE_F** — The elevation (in feet above sea level) of the aquifer’s base at a given location. Sample value: `1300` means the aquifer starts 1,300 feet above sea level there.  
**DK_BASE_** — A numeric identifier for specific segments of the aquifer’s base. Sample value: `54` corresponds to a mapped section of the aquifer.  

## Try it yourself  
Check the aquifer’s depth and elevation near your property using [Kansas’s interactive groundwater map](https://www.kgs.ku.edu/) to estimate well-drilling costs.
