## What this tracks  
This dataset tracks pollution levels and water quality issues in Kansas lakes, identifying specific contaminants that exceed safe limits.

## Why it matters to you  
Before planning a fishing trip or letting your kids swim in a Kansas lake, check this data to see if the water has high levels of harmful substances like E. coli or mercury. If you're a farmer near Norton Lake, the "EUTROPHICATION: High" warning tells you fertilizer runoff may be worsening algae blooms downstream. Anglers can use the "DISSOLVED_OXYGEN: Low" alerts to avoid fish kills in oxygen-deprived waters.

## How to read this data  
**LAKE_NAME** — Official lake names, sometimes with alternate names in parentheses (e.g., "Norton Lake (Sebelius Lake)").  
**DISSOLVED_OXYGEN** — Measures oxygen levels fish need to survive; "Low" values mean potential fish kills.  
**EUTROPHICATION** — Rates nutrient pollution that causes algae blooms; "High" means visible green scum likely.  
**PH** — Indicates acidity; "Low" values mean corrosive water that may damage boats or irritate skin.  

## Try it yourself  
Search for your nearest fishing spot by lake name at [KDHE's TMDL portal](https://maps.kdhe.state.ks.us/tmdlreports/) to see if it has active contamination warnings.
