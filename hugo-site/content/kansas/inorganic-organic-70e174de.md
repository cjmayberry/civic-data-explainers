## What this tracks  
This dataset records groundwater contamination levels from private and public wells in Kansas, testing for radioactive elements like uranium and radium between 1985 and 2001.  

## Why it matters to you  
If you rely on well water in Kansas, this data reveals whether your area historically had unsafe radiation levels—helping you decide whether to test your current water supply or install filtration. Homebuyers could check this before purchasing property with a well, and farmers might use it to assess irrigation risks.  

## How to read this data  
**SITE_NAME** — Identifies the well location (e.g., "SG04WHITEWATER#02").  
**Gross_Uranium_pCi/L** — Measures uranium contamination (sample: 15 pCi/L; EPA's limit is 30 pCi/L).  
**Radon-222_pCi/L** — Shows radon levels (sample: 685 pCi/L; EPA recommends action above 300 pCi/L).  
**COUNTY** — Two-letter county code (e.g., "BU" for Butler County).  

## Try it yourself  
Search the dataset by your county code (like "BU" for Butler) to see if wells near you were flagged for radiation. If so, consider testing your water.
