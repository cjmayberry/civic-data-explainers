## What this tracks  
This dataset records radioactive elements like uranium and radium in Kansas groundwater wells, sampled between 1985 and 2001.

## Why it matters to you  
If you rely on well water in Kansas, this data shows whether your area historically had elevated radiation levels. A homeowner might test their water or install filtration if their well draws from an aquifer with high radon (685 pCi/L in one sample). Farmers could use this to assess irrigation water safety for crops. Note that newer wells aren’t included—this is a snapshot of older test results.

## How to read this data  
**SITE_NAME** — The well identifier, like "SG04WHITEWATER#02".  
**Gross_Uranium_pCi/L** — Uranium concentration; sample shows 15 picocuries per liter (the EPA limit is 30).  
**Radon-222_pCi/L** — Radon gas levels; one sample hit 685 pCi/L (EPA recommends action above 300).  
**AQUIFER** — The groundwater source, like "Alluvial" or "Dakota" sandstone.  

## Try it yourself  
Check if your county (like "BU" for Butler County) has test results by searching the dataset for nearby **SITE_NAME** entries or matching **LEGAL_TRS** land survey codes.
