## What this tracks  
This dataset measures the depth, elevation, and water quality of the Dakota Aquifer beneath Kansas, including total dissolved solids in groundwater.

## Why it matters to you  
If you rely on well water in Kansas, this data tells you how deep you’ll need to drill to reach the Dakota Aquifer and whether the water is likely safe for drinking or irrigation (based on dissolved solids). Farmers can use it to plan crop irrigation, and rural homeowners can estimate well-drilling costs before purchasing property.

## How to read this data  
**ALTITUDE_F** — Elevation (in feet above sea level) of the aquifer’s top surface; sample value is 1300 (higher numbers mean shallower drilling).  
**DK_BASE_** — Depth (in feet) to the base of the aquifer; sample value is 54 (deeper values may require more expensive drilling).  

## Try it yourself  
Check the aquifer’s depth and elevation near your property using the [Kansas Geological Survey’s interactive map](https://www.kgs.ku.edu/) to estimate well-drilling feasibility.
