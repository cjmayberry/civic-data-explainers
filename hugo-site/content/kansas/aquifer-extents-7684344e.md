## What this tracks  
This dataset maps the boundaries of major underground water sources (aquifers) across Kansas.

## Why it matters to you  
If you're drilling a well for your home or farm, this data shows whether you're likely to hit usable groundwater beneath your property. Farmers in western Kansas can check if their land sits above the High Plains Aquifer before investing in irrigation systems. Developers in Wichita can verify if proposed construction sites overlap protected aquifer recharge zones.

## How to read this data  
**NAME** — Identifies the aquifer (e.g., "Ozark Aquifer"). Kansas has 12 major aquifers including the Dakota and High Plains systems.  
**Shape__Area** — Technical field showing the aquifer's geographic size in square units (not meaningful for most residents).  

## Try it yourself  
Zoom to your property on the Kansas Geological Survey's interactive map to see if it overlies one of these aquifers before planning any groundwater projects.
