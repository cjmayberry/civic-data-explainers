## What this tracks  
This dataset maps Kansas' watershed boundaries using 8-digit Hydrologic Unit Codes (HUCs), which divide land areas based on water flow patterns.

## Why it matters to you  
If you're buying property near a creek or planning a farm irrigation system, this data shows which watershed your land sits in—critical for understanding flood risks and water rights. Developers in Kansas City can check HUC boundaries to anticipate stormwater runoff regulations. Homeowners near the Missouri-Nishnabotna basin can identify upstream pollution sources affecting their local water quality.

## How to read this data  
**HUC_8** — An 8-digit code like "10240005" representing a specific sub-watershed, often used in permitting (e.g., well drilling applications).  
**HU_8_STATE** — Lists states the watershed crosses (e.g., "IA KS MO NE"), important for interstate water agreements.  
**SQ_MILES** — Shows the watershed's size (e.g., "1701"), helping gauge potential flood impact areas.  

## Try it yourself  
Search your address on Kansas' [HUC lookup map](https://hub.kansasgis.org/) to see if your property is in the Tarkio-Wolf or another sub-basin.
