## What this is  
This dataset tracks special planning areas in Oklahoma City that aren't covered by normal zoning rules, like closed streets or vacated lots.

## Why it matters to you  
These non-zoning areas can affect what gets built near you or how land gets used. For example, if a street near your home is marked as "vacated," it might mean the city plans to sell that land for development. Businesses checking locations might find some parcels have special restrictions that zoning maps don't show.

## How to read this data  
Key fields include:  
- **Zoning Class**: Short code showing why this area is exempt from normal zoning (like "VAC" for vacated land).  
- **Description**: Plain-English details (e.g., "Former railroad right-of-way").  
- **Ordinance Number**: The city law that created this exception, if applicable.  

*Note: The dataset doesn’t explain what zoning codes like "VAC" mean—you’d need to cross-reference city documents.*

## Where this leaves you  
While you can’t look up your address here, this data helps explain why some areas might develop differently than their zoning suggests. Check the city’s zoning maps *and* this dataset for the full picture.
