## What this tracks  
This dataset records weekly counts of reported communicable diseases in Missouri, comparing current numbers to historical averages.

## Why it matters to you  
If you're planning outdoor activities in Missouri, this data tells you whether disease risks like anaplasmosis (transmitted by ticks) are spiking in your area. Parents can check if childhood illnesses are circulating before playdates, and healthcare workers can anticipate potential outbreaks. The rate-per-100k value shows whether your community is experiencing above-normal transmission.

## How to read this data  
**disease_or_condition** — The specific illness being tracked, like "ANAPLASMOSIS" in the sample.  
**current_week_ytd** — Total cases reported this week (sample shows 35 anaplasmosis cases).  
**rate_per_100k** — How common the disease is per 100,000 residents (0.57 means low risk in the sample).  
**_5_yr_median** — Typical case count for this time of year (11 cases in the sample, suggesting current numbers are higher than usual).

## Where this leaves you  
This dataset doesn't let you look anything up by your address, but it's still a window into how the state operates. Read it to understand the scope of the program and what the official records contain; the city or state's own interactive map (linked on this page) shows the locations involved.
